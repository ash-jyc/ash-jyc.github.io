Coffee in my version of Paradise

Created on November 14, 2026

2026   ·   life

By some “I’m so done with work” and “literally going home next week” miracle, I’ve managed to physically split the attachment between my hip and my laptop, physically dragging myself to the local legend of a coffee shop, Caffe Paradiso. A change of pace from the banalities of the subdivision of my desk.

Due to the usual circumstances of seeing the same suspects every single day, I’ve run out of topics to converse about. “How was your day?” or “Anything interesting happen recently?” become moot questions as I only require copying and pasting the answer from the previous day. As a response, I’ve decided to pose the deeper life questions to the people around me:

“What is something interesting you think about?”

I would expect myself to think of research more, but that isn’t so much the case. Recently, I’ve explored the age-old topic of interpersonal relationships, or more specifically, how my friendships are changing as an adult. In high school and college, the close proximity of physical distance greatly aided in the close proximity of my friendships. Maintaining that friendship when the physical distance lengthens becomes difficult. Technology closes that gap, but the instantaneous sending of a reel in an incompatible timezone will never replace the sixth sense honed from years of growing together.

The dark eye bags from working on a project no one appreciates, and the strained smiles from wanting to laugh with only the power of Red Bull to do so. Or the sparkles in the eyes when the boyfriend is paying, and the ringing beratement of the unfairness of our social constructs. Knowledge once learned, now drifting, drifting away.

I spend an effort keeping in touch with friends because it’s one of my core beliefs. I believe it to be important, to selflessly and relentlessly remember the people who have given so much to me.

But at the same time, priorities change, and people change. That is the natural course of life.

My priorities right now are research, and I spend a bit of my time positioning myself within research and academia. Put bluntly, academia is broken. Human reviewers cannot keep up with the speed of AI slop. Reviewers use AI to review the work of AI. And the loop continues, removing more and more human effort at every iteration. Consequently, I’ve been implored to think about the relationship between myself and AI.

I don’t have a strong stance for whether humans or AIs are better for research. Maybe humans are better now at generating new ideas to feed into AI. Maybe there will always be a place for human researchers. But there’s no denying AI will continue improving, radically changing the current research landscape.

For me, it’s important that I acknowledge the state of AI, its presence in both my own research and the reality of others. In my own research, it still seems to be the case I have to baby my AI and tell it exactly what to do and not to do. I thoroughly enjoy this process, frequently offering design feedback about why a pipeline is designed in a certain way and asking why it makes sense. I still adhere to one of my life goals, to always do what I love, and I feel that I’m still there.

In general, AI seeks to displace many functions one by one: math, science, cybersecurity, economics, etc. One of the UIUC professors put this best: “What a time to be alive.” These are both exciting and scary times. For everyone, it’s the road that has yet to be taken. All we can do is take it and have fun while doing so.

My current roads will end up leading me back to my laptop and attaching us at the hip. Because unfortunately, my AI requires babysitting and has yet to fully replace me. There’s one thing that AI will never be able to do though: enjoy the Dalgona coffee from Caffe Paradiso and supercharge the ramblings of a sleepless philosopher.
